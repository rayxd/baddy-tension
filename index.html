    How it works & tips

    The tight string bed rings like a drum skin. Tighter strings ring higher (about 1177 Hz at 24 lbs up to 1362 Hz at 30 lbs on a typical racket), so the app measures the pitch of your tap and works backwards using the racket's head size and string gauge.

    • Tap the centre of the strings. A fingernail flick or the butt of another racket works well.
    • Measure somewhere quiet, with the phone 5–20 cm from the strings.
    • Calibrate right after a fresh restring. Set the date strung to today when you edit the racket, and the app offers to calibrate on your next reading. This corrects for your racket's frame and string, and makes later readings far more accurate.
    • The ± after a reading shows how closely your taps agreed. If they disagree, the app asks for another tap.
    • Swipe left or right on the racket to switch rackets, or tap the racket name at the top.
    • The string gauge matters most: tension scales with gauge squared, so pick the right string.
    • Expect about 5–10% loss in the first day after stringing. That's normal.
    • Using the same racket over time, the trend is more reliable than any single number.

    Racket

    Head size & string gauge

    These are filled in from your racket and string choice. Head sizes are approximate, because makers don't publish string-bed dimensions. For a racket that isn't listed, measure the inside of the strung area from top to throat (length) and side to side (width).

    Look

    Stock colours are close to the maker's colourway. Logos and printed graphics aren't shown. Change any colour to match your own racket.
